# B19.7 — Provenance, Retrieval Traceability, and Content Integrity

## 1. Overview

B19.7 establishes provenance and integrity controls for the Tawanitech
Automation Platform (TAP) RAG knowledge layer.

B19.3 established controlled knowledge ingestion.

B19.4 established deterministic evidence indexing.

B19.7 extends the knowledge lifecycle with:

- Document provenance
- Retrieval provenance
- Source traceability
- Version tracking
- Content integrity metadata
- SHA-256 content checksums
- Controlled checksum verification during ingestion

The purpose is to ensure that knowledge used by future retrieval and AI
investigation can be traced back to its source and, when integrity
verification is requested, validated against its recorded content hash.

B19.7 does not implement:

- Embeddings
- Vector search
- Semantic retrieval
- AI reasoning
- Authorization
- Infrastructure execution

---

## 3. Document Provenance

Document provenance answers:

> Where did this knowledge come from?

`KnowledgeProvenance` records:

- `source`
- `source_type`
- `collected_at`
- `collector`
- `version`
- `checksum`
- `reference`

Provenance is informational metadata.

It does not grant authority to execute actions.

---

## 4. Retrieval Provenance

Document provenance and retrieval provenance are intentionally separate.

### Document provenance

Describes the origin of the knowledge.

Example:

    Source:
        docs/runbooks/example-service.md

    Collector:
        server-b-tap-b19

    Version:
        1.0

### Retrieval provenance

Describes how the knowledge was retrieved.

`KnowledgeRetrievalRecord` records:

- `retrieval_id`
- `document_id`
- `retrieved_at`
- `retrieval_method`
- `source`

Therefore:

    Document Provenance
        |
        +-- Where did the knowledge originate?
        |
        v
    Retrieval Provenance
        |
        +-- How and when was it retrieved?

This distinction is important for future RAG auditability.

## 5. Content Integrity

B19.7 introduces SHA-256 content checksums.

The checksum is calculated from the knowledge document content:

    Knowledge Content
          |
          v
    SHA-256
          |
          v
    sha256:<64 hexadecimal characters>

The implementation provides:

- `calculate_content_checksum()`
- `verify_content_checksum()`

Example:

    checksum = calculate_content_checksum(document.content)

The checksum provides a deterministic representation of the content
at the time it was hashed.

If the content changes, the calculated checksum changes.

---

## 6. Integrity Verification at Ingestion

B19.7.6 integrates checksum verification into the controlled ingestion
boundary.

The ingestion API provides:

    verify_integrity=False

by default for backward compatibility with existing B19.3 knowledge
producers.

When integrity verification is explicitly enabled:

    verify_integrity=True

the following rules apply:

### Valid checksum

    Document content
          |
          v
    Calculate SHA-256
          |
          v
    Compare with provenance.checksum
          |
          v
        MATCH
          |
          v
       ACCEPT

### Invalid checksum

    Document content
          |
          v
    Calculate SHA-256
          |
          v
    Compare with provenance.checksum
          |
          v
       MISMATCH
          |
          v
        REJECT

### Missing checksum

When integrity verification is enabled and no checksum is supplied:

    REJECT

The ingestion boundary raises a validation error.

---

## 7. Backward Compatibility

B19.7.6 intentionally does not make checksum verification mandatory
for every existing ingestion call.

Existing calls remain valid:

    ingestor.ingest(
        document,
        provenance,
    )

Controlled integrity verification is explicit:

    ingestor.ingest(
        document,
        provenance,
        verify_integrity=True,
    )

This allows existing B19.3 knowledge producers to remain compatible
while establishing an integrity-verification capability for controlled
ingestion paths.

A future production policy may require checksums for selected knowledge
sources.

## 8. Evidence Index Provenance

B19.7 extends the evidence index so provenance information survives
indexing.

`EvidenceIndexEntry` preserves:

- `provenance_reference`
- `provenance_version`
- `provenance_checksum`
- `provenance_collected_at`
- `provenance_collector`

The flow is therefore:

    KnowledgeDocument
          |
          v
    KnowledgeProvenance
          |
          v
    IngestedKnowledge
          |
          v
    EvidenceIndex
          |
          v
    Provenance-preserving index entry

The index does not become an authority boundary.

It remains a deterministic information and navigation layer.

## 10. Critical Conceptual Separation

TAP distinguishes three different concepts:

    OBSERVED EVIDENCE
        |
        | What was observed from the infrastructure?
        v

    RETRIEVED KNOWLEDGE
        |
        | What relevant knowledge was retrieved?
        v

    AI REASONING
        |
        | What interpretation or hypothesis did the model produce?
        v

These must not be treated as the same thing.

Evidence establishes what happened.

Retrieved knowledge supplies relevant context.

AI reasoning interprets evidence and knowledge.

TAP governance remains responsible for authority and consequential
actions.

---

## 11. TAP Governance Boundary

B19 provenance and integrity controls do not grant execution authority.

The architecture remains:

    AI
     |
     | proposes / reasons
     v
    TAP
     |
     | policy / authorization
     v
    Job Broker
     |
     | governed execution
     v
    Automation
     |
     v
    Verification
     |
     v
    Recorder

Therefore:

> Provenance establishes traceability. It does not establish authority.

> A valid checksum establishes content integrity at verification time. It
> does not authorize an infrastructure action.

## 13. Security and Audit Considerations

Checksum verification helps detect content changes but does not by
itself prove that a source is trustworthy.

Provenance should therefore be interpreted together with:

- Source identity
- Collector identity
- Collection time
- Version information
- Evidence relationships
- Retrieval method
- Future access-control policy
- Future Recorder/audit records

SHA-256 is used here as a deterministic content-integrity mechanism.

It is not a replacement for:

- Authentication
- Authorization
- Digital signatures
- Secure transport
- Trusted source validation

These controls may be introduced at later stages where required.

## 16. Architectural Principle

B19.7 reinforces the following AI-SRE principle:

> Evidence establishes what happened. RAG supplies relevant knowledge.
> Provenance establishes where that knowledge came from. Integrity
> verification establishes whether the content matches its recorded
> checksum. AI investigates and reasons. TAP governs decisions and
> authority.

This keeps the knowledge layer informative and traceable while
preserving TAP as the authority boundary.
